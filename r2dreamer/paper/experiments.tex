\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate MWM on proprioceptive DeepMind Control \texttt{walker-walk} with a single offline checkpoint (R2-Dreamer, ${\sim}510$K environment steps pretraining) held fixed across all test-time runs.
Unless stated otherwise, plastic uses $\eta=0.05$, $\lambda_0=10^{-3}$, $\mu=2$, and \texttt{gate\_mode=always\_on}; the evaluation protocol follows Section~\ref{sec:protocol} ($\mathcal{A}_1/\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}_2$ with $\gamma \in \{1.0, 0.7, 1.0\}$).
We report mean $\pm$ std over seeds where applicable and paired per-seed comparisons when two conditions share the same random seed and shift realization.

Our empirical program addresses six questions:
\textbf{(Q1)} Does the agent detect gain shift in world-model error?
\textbf{(Q2)} Does fast plasticity improve latent alignment under shift?
\textbf{(Q3)} Does fast plasticity alone improve control?
\textbf{(Q4)} Does online $P_t$ help slow actor adaptation?
\textbf{(Q5)} What actor-adapt schedule and imagination mode work best?
\textbf{(Q6)} Does consolidation drain $P_t$ and improve slow-store learning?

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Setup and baselines}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Checkpoint and shift.}
All experiments load the same pretrained weights and freeze them for inference (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}).
Shift is actuator gain $\gamma=0.7$ during $\mathcal{B}$ only; MuJoCo dynamics are unchanged.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Frozen}: no $P_t$, no actor or WM updates ($\pi_\theta$ and $g$ fixed).
  \item \textbf{Plastic-only}: online $P_t$ (Section~\ref{sec:fast-plastic}), frozen actor.
  \item \textbf{Plastic + actor (cfg0)}: online $P_t$ plus slow actor fine-tuning ($\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}{=}50$, $E{=}4$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{actor}}{=}1$, frozen-WM imagine).
  \item \textbf{Actor w/o online plastic (C)}: actor adapt identical to cfg0 but $P_t$ inference disabled during $\mathcal{B}$.
  \item \textbf{Consolidation A--D}: Table~\ref{tab:conditions} with slow $\theta_{\mathrm{wm}}$ and flush ablations.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Seeds and compute.}
Main act and actor-adapt sweeps use $10$ random seeds ($0,\ldots,9$).
WM-imagination and consolidation pilots use $3$ seeds ($0,2,5$) due to cost; we mark these as pilots and do not over-interpret small-$n$ significance.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Shift detection and fast-store dynamics (Q1--Q2)}
\label{sec:exp-detection}

\paragraph{World-model error rises at shift.}
Across plastic runs, prior prediction error $\mathrm{MSE}_t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:signed-error}) increases from $\mathcal{A}_1$ to $\mathcal{B}$ and the fast trace norm grows from near zero to the cap $\mu=2$ within tens of steps after $\gamma$ drops.
This pattern is consistent across seeds and visible in per-step TensorBoard logs for consolidation runs.
Figure~\ref{fig:trace-curve} (supplementary) plots $\|P_t\|_F$ for Condition~C versus A/B/D.

\paragraph{Plastic vs.\ frozen prior MSE (10 seeds, plastic-only).}
Table~\ref{tab:plastic-act} compares Phase~$\mathcal{B}$ return and terminal prior MSE (mean of last $20$ steps).
Fast plasticity \emph{lowers} prior MSE relative to frozen in $6/10$ seeds; aggregate prior-MSE advantage is seed-skewed (large improvements on seeds $0,5,6$; degradations on $1,2,3,5$).

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Plastic-only vs.\ frozen inference ($n{=}10$ seeds, Phase~$\mathcal{B}$). $\Delta r$ is plastic minus frozen mean return; $\Delta_{\mathrm{MSE}}$ is frozen minus plastic prior MSE (last 20), so positive favors plastic.}
  \label{tab:plastic-act}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Metric & Value \\
    \midrule
    Mean $\Delta r$ (plastic $-$ frozen) & $-0.009 \pm 0.21$ \\
    Seeds with $\Delta r > 0$ & $4/10$ \\
    Seeds with $\Delta_{\mathrm{MSE}} > 0$ & $6/10$ \\
    Mean $\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}_1$ return ratio (plastic) & $0.84$ \\
    Mean $\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}_1$ return ratio (frozen) & $0.78$ \\
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Interpretation (Q1--Q2).}
\textbf{Detection} is supported: shift induces sustained prediction error and saturates $P_t$.
\textbf{WM alignment} under fast plasticity is \emph{partially} supported---more seeds improve than worsen, but effect size varies and mean prior-MSE gap is not uniformly positive.
Plasticity also \emph{attenuates reward drop} during $\mathcal{B}$ on average (higher $\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}_1$ ratio than frozen), even when it does not win pairwise return on every seed.

\paragraph{Gate ablation (prior MSE only).}
A $10$-seed sweep over gate modes (\texttt{always\_on}, \texttt{surprise\_only}, \texttt{selective}) shows \texttt{surprise\_only} ($\eta=0.1$) achieves the highest prior-MSE win rate ($8/10$) against frozen, while \texttt{always\_on}---used in main runs---wins $7/10$.
We retain \texttt{always\_on} for downstream actor experiments to maximize continuous fast-store activity; selective gating may improve WM metrics but is not re-tuned jointly with actor adapt here.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Fast plasticity alone does not reliably improve control (Q3)}
\label{sec:exp-plastic-only}

Plastic-only inference does \emph{not} establish a robust control benefit: mean Phase~$\mathcal{B}$ return is statistically indistinguishable from frozen at $n{=}10$ (Table~\ref{tab:plastic-act}).
Best-case seeds are substantial (e.g., seed~$0$: $\Delta r {=} +0.35$), but negative seeds (e.g., seed~$1$: $\Delta r {=} -0.28$) offset the average.

\paragraph{Recovery phase $\mathcal{A}_2$.}
When gain returns to $1.0$, both plastic and frozen recover to near-pre-shift return ($\mathcal{A}_2/\mathcal{A}_1 \approx 1.06$ frozen, $1.14$ plastic), indicating that $\mathcal{B}$-phase adaptation leaves carry-over into $\mathcal{A}_2$ without catastrophic drift in this protocol.

\paragraph{Latency.}
Rolling-window recovery time to $90\%$ of $\mathcal{A}_1$ return during $\mathcal{B}$ is \emph{seed-dependent} and not consistently faster for plastic-only (e.g., seed~$0$: plastic step $74$ vs.\ frozen $41$).
We therefore do \emph{not} claim a universal speed advantage of Hebbian plasticity on reward recovery without slow actor learning.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Online plasticity scaffolds slow actor adaptation (Q4)}
\label{sec:exp-bc}

We next test whether live $P_t$ during $\mathcal{B}$ improves the \emph{slow} pathway: cfg0 actor adaptation with vs.\ without online plastic (Conditions B vs.\ C in the actor-adapt ablation, $n{=}10$).

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Online $P_t$ during actor adapt (Phase~$\mathcal{B}$, $n{=}10$). B: plastic on; C: plastic off.}
  \label{tab:bc}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    & B (online $P_t$) & C (no $P_t$) \\
    \midrule
    Mean return & $1.386$ & $1.318$ \\
    Mean $\Delta r$ (B $-$ C) & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$+0.068 \pm 0.10$} \\
    Seeds with $\Delta r > 0$ & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$7/10$} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

This is the clearest \emph{control} benefit attributable to the fast pathway in our stack: $P_t$ does not need to directly maximize return if it shapes the replay distribution and latent beliefs on which actor gradients are computed.
Per-seed prior-MSE gaps between B and C are heterogeneous; online plastic is not a strict DOM improvement on WM error in every seed, but it wins on return in $70\%$ of paired runs.

\subsection{Actor schedule and imagination ablations (Q5)}
\label{sec:exp-actor-sweep}

\paragraph{Imagination dynamics ($n{=}3$ pilot).}
Using cfg0 actor updates, we compare plastic-WM vs.\ frozen-WM imagination (Section~\ref{sec:slow-actor}).
Plastic-WM imagine \emph{hurts} Phase~$\mathcal{B}$ return in all three seeds (mean $\Delta r = -0.13$; plastic WM never wins).
The recommended stack keeps imagination on the frozen prior while maintaining online $P_t$ during environment interaction and replay encoding.

\paragraph{Adapt schedule sweep ($n{=}10$ for top configs).}
Table~\ref{tab:cfg-sweep} summarizes Phase~$\mathcal{B}$ return for cfg0 (winner), a conservative schedule (cfg1), and no actor adapt.
A $3$-seed pilot of aggressive diversity (cfg2) and micro-updates (cfg3) underperforms cfg0 and is excluded from full-scale runs.

\paragraph{Takeaway (Q5).}
Control gains come from \textbf{frozen-WM imagine + online $P_t$ + cfg0 actor cadence}.
cfg0 beats plastic-only act in $6/10$ seeds (mean $+0.05$) and beats no-adapt in $4/10$ (mean $+0.02$); variance across seeds remains high (std ${\approx} 0.14$).

\subsection{Consolidation pilot: flush without reward transfer (Q6)}
\label{sec:exp-consolidation}

We run Conditions A--D (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}) with slow $\theta_{\mathrm{wm}}$ every $100$ steps, $\alpha_{\mathrm{wm}}{=}0.02$, and cfg0 actor adapt ($n{=}3$ seeds).

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Consolidation pilot ($n{=}3$, Phase~$\mathcal{B}$).}
  \label{tab:consolidation}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Cond. & Mean return & $\|P_t\|_F$ (last 20) & Prior MSE (last 20) \\
    \midrule
    A baseline      & $1.357 \pm 0.22$ & $2.00$ & $5.91$ \\
    B slow $\theta$ only & $\mathbf{1.409 \pm 0.16}$ & $2.00$ & $5.03$ \\
    C consolidate   & $1.359 \pm 0.05$ & $\mathbf{0.51}$ & $6.06$ \\
    D flush trap    & $1.408 \pm 0.13$ & $2.00$ & $4.71$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
Condition~C alone collapses $\|P_t\|_F$ from cap $2.0$ to $0.33$--$0.75$ by end of $\mathcal{B}$ in all three seeds, while A/B/D remain saturated.
Coupled flush boost $b_t$ rises only in C; $\lambda^{\mathrm{eff}}$ increases in tandem (TensorBoard).
This confirms the \emph{fast-store drainage} mechanism (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:lambda-eff}--\eqref{eq:plastic-proj}).

\paragraph{What is not yet shown.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Reward:} C does not beat A ($1.359$ vs.\ $1.357$); B (slow $\theta$ without flush) achieves the highest pilot return.
  \item \textbf{Stable slow loss:} dynamics KL during $\theta_{\mathrm{wm}}$ updates oscillates across seeds; monotonic ``$\theta$ learns, then $P_t$ retires'' is not clean.
  \item \textbf{Trap:} D (flush without slow $\theta$) does not drain $P_t$ and does not consistently harm return---the negative control is partial.
  \item \textbf{Scale:} $n{=}3$; $10$-seed consolidation with retuned $(\alpha_{\mathrm{wm}}, \Delta_{\mathrm{slow}}, b_t$ ramp$)$ is future work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:exp-summary}

Table~\ref{tab:summary-rq} maps research questions to outcomes.
MWM is best understood as a \emph{multi-timescale} system: fast $P_t$ provides immediate WM reactivity and improves slow actor learning when present online; slow actor updates deliver the primary reliable control gains; consolidation drains $P_t$ as designed but has not yet translated into improved return at pilot scale.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Research questions and experimental verdicts (walker gain shift).}
  \label{tab:summary-rq}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.52\linewidth}p{0.40\linewidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Question & Verdict \\
    \midrule
    Q1: Detect shift? & \textbf{Yes} (prior MSE $\uparrow$, $\|P_t\|$ saturates) \\
    Q2: Fast WM alignment? & \textbf{Partial} ($6/10$ seeds, variable magnitude) \\
    Q3: Plastic-only control? & \textbf{No} (mean $\Delta r \approx 0$, $4/10$ win) \\
    Q4: Scaffold actor adapt? & \textbf{Yes} ($7/10$, mean $+0.068$) \\
    Q5: Best slow stack? & \textbf{frozen imagine + cfg0} ($r_{\mathcal{B}}{=}1.46$) \\
    Q6: Consolidation / flush? & \textbf{Mechanism yes}, \textbf{reward no} (pilot $n{=}3$) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Results are confined to one proprioceptive task and one shift family (actuator gain).
We do not evaluate visual observations, physical parameter shifts, or lifelong deployment beyond the $1500$-step protocol.
Recovery-time claims are not robust for plastic-only.
Consolidation hyperparameters used in the pilot ($\Delta_{\mathrm{slow}}{=}100$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{wm}}{=}0.02$) were not re-optimized after observing premature flush and unstable slow loss.
Finally, reward and value heads remain frozen during slow WM updates; mismatch between adapted dynamics and frozen reward modeling may cap consolidation benefits.
